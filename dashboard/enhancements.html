CHAPTER 04 / 04 BEYOND THE BASELINE

A small real gain, and a smarter tradeoff.

Tuning genuinely helped this time. A threshold trick helps even more, if you're willing to trade a little accuracy for it.

Experiments

4 APPROACHES TESTED
APPROACH ACCURACY vs. BASELINE
Random Forest (baseline) 91.34% — DEPLOYED
RandomizedSearchCV tuning 91.39% ▲ +0.05pp
GridSearchCV tuning 91.46% ▲ +0.12pp — BEST
CatBoost 86.33% ▼ −5.01pp
Stacking Ensemble (tuned RF + XGB + DT + CatBoost) 90.72% ▼ −0.62pp

This time, tuning helped — a small, real gain. Both searches used a 40,000-sample subset just to find good hyperparameters quickly, then retrained on the full 131,192-sample balanced training set — the fix that made this result trustworthy. CatBoost and the stacking ensemble still fell short of the simple baseline.

Why The Baseline Is Still Deployed

0.12 PERCENTAGE POINTS

The tuned model's edge is real but tiny — 0.12 percentage points, close to normal run-to-run noise. The baseline was already live before this tuning result came in. Swapping to the tuned version is a small, low-risk upgrade — tracked below, not urgent.

Trading Accuracy For Recall

CLASSIFICATION THRESHOLD
THRESHOLD ACCURACY HAZARD PRECISION HAZARD RECALL HAZARD F1
0.50 (default) 91.46% 56.2% 55.4% 55.8%
0.45 90.81% 52.5% 59.5% 55.8%
0.40 90.10% 49.3% 64.1% 55.8%
0.35 89.16% 46.2% 69.7% 55.6%
0.30 88.11% 43.5% 74.2% 54.8%

Lowering the threshold from 0.50 to 0.30 pushes hazard recall from 55% to 74% — catching far more real hazards — at the cost of more false alarms and 3.4 points of overall accuracy. F1 barely moves, which means this isn't a free win, it's a genuine tradeoff. For a safety-first use case, catching more real threats may be worth more than a slightly higher accuracy number.
