CHAPTER 04 / 04 BEYOND THE BASELINE

What we tried to beat Random Forest — and what won.

Tuning and ensembling don't automatically win. Here's the honest comparison, including the attempts that underperformed the baseline.

Experiments

4 APPROACHES TESTED
APPROACH ACCURACY vs. BASELINE
Random Forest (baseline) 91.34% — DEPLOYED
RandomizedSearchCV tuning 85.99% ▼ −5.35pp
GridSearchCV tuning 86.00% ▼ −5.34pp
CatBoost 86.33% ▼ −5.01pp
Stacking Ensemble (RF-tuned + XGB + DT + CatBoost) 88.75% ▼ −2.59pp

None of the enhancement attempts beat the baseline — and that's a real, useful finding, not a failure to hide. The likely cause: both search methods tuned on a 40,000-sample subset of the training data rather than the full ~131,000-sample balanced set the baseline trained on. Less data during tuning meant the "improved" hyperparameters were optimized for a smaller, less representative slice — a good reminder that tuning strategy matters as much as the tuning itself.

Why Stacking Came Closest

88.75% — BEST OF THE ENHANCEMENTS

The stacking ensemble combined the tuned Random Forest, XGBoost, Decision Tree, and CatBoost as base learners, with a Logistic Regression meta-model learning how to weight their combined votes. It outperformed every individual enhancement attempt — ensembling recovered some of what individual tuning lost — but still fell short of the simple baseline trained on the full dataset. This points to the same root cause: the base learners feeding the stack were themselves trained on the smaller tuning subset.
