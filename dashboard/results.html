Random Forest won outright on accuracy and F1, and is the model actually deployed behind the live tracker. Notice KNN and GaussianNB post higher precision than Random Forest but far lower recall — they're more conservative, missing more real hazardous objects to avoid false alarms. For a hazard-detection task, recall matters: a missed hazardous object is a worse failure than a false alarm, which is part of why Random Forest's balance of the two made it the right choice, not just its top-line accuracy.

Of 1,768 genuinely hazardous objects in the test set, 1,326 were correctly flagged (75% recall on the hazard class) — 442 were missed. Of the objects flagged as hazardous, 39% actually were, meaning most alerts are false positives. This is the classic precision/recall tradeoff of a heavily imbalanced problem: tuned toward not missing threats, at the cost of more false alarms.

An AUC of 0.923 indicates strong separability between the two classes overall. The cross-validation mean (91.1%) closely tracks the deployed model's test accuracy (91.3%) — a good sign the result isn't a lucky split. One fold dipped to 84.5%, worth keeping in mind as a sign of some sensitivity to which data lands in training vs. validation.
